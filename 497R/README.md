## Overview
ME EN 497R is a research course that may be applied toward the technical elective requirement for the Mechanical Engineering program (other course numbers are used in Physics, Math, etc.).
The overall goal of the 497R course is to give undergraduates research experiences typically reserved for graduate students, albeit in an format accessible to the abilities and time constraints of an undergraduate student.
We have formalized the structure while allowing a great deal of student-lead learning.
497R is a challenging course that requires self-motivation.

The course is taught by a graduate student in our lab. To begin the course, you must first complete the onboarding project, meet with your graduate mentor, determine which track you'd like to pursue, and get an add code from Dr. Ning. 

## Learning Objectives
1) Develop the disposition of a researcher
2) Develop theoretical knowledge in a specific area
3) Develop research skills

### Disposition of a Researcher
The amount of content that someone needs to be familiar with in order to work in the FLOW Lab is significant. We can't possibly teach you everything you need to know, especially in a single semester. Therefore, our primary learning objective is to help you develop the character traits (or disposition in education-speak) that will enable you to be successful. 

A disposition of a researcher is a slightly abstract concept. Someone who has the disposition of a researcher dives into problems and new concepts, applies critical-thinking to their analyses, is continually learning, demonstrates tenacity in working through complex topics, is self-managed (independent), and keeps organized notes. If you think about someone who is "on it", or "has their life together", this is our primary learning objective (at least when it comes to doing research and engineering). 

To help communicate what we are looking for we've broken up this objective into three areas:
1) Researchers independently pursue the knowledge and skills requisite for engineering problem-solving. 
2) Researchers demonstrate maturity in their analysis skills. 
3) Researchers are intrinsically motivated to do their work. 

Don't be afraid if you feel like you are at this level yet. The course is designed to help you develop these traits. Since this course is taught one-on-one by a graduate student in our lab, it is a unique opportunity for us to help you grow. As previously alluded to, students who succeed at this learning objective tend to be successful in all other learning objectives.

## Course Structure

### Project Tracks
The FLOW Lab's version of the 497R is designed to be tailored to student's interests. As such, when beginning the course, the student chooses a project track. The track is a collection of assignments that focuses on a specific topic. Every track will include aspects of design optimization (since that is a major component of our research). 

Current tracks are:
- Airframe Design
- Rotor Design

Tracks under development:
- Airfoil Design
- Surrogate modeling
- Windfarm Design

### Course Outline (16 Weeks)
Each track is broken up into 8 assignments that should take a maximum of 2 weeks to complete. 

### Grading
Your grade is split into two major portions 1) time spent on the course, and 2) grades from your assignments. Each portion accounts for 50% of your grade. 

#### Time Spent grade
To get full points are expected to spend an average of 10 hours a week working on this class. The grade is a direct percentage of your semester average (if you average 9 hours across the semester, then you get a 90% on this portion (making your max overall grade a 95%)). 

Your hours will be reported on a google sheet shared with your mentor. You and your mentor should go over your hours on a bi-weekly basis. Lab meetings and mentor meetings may be included in your time spent. 

We have found that to do well in this course, 10 hours is a minimum. If you want to earn an A, expect to spend more than 10 hours each week. 

#### Assignment grades
Your graduate student mentor will grade all 8 assignments that you'll do across the semester. While your graduate student mentor has full discretion over this portion of your grade, they will compare your work with standards developed by the lab. They should provide useful feedback in order to help you improve. 

We recognize that the topics presented in this class can be difficult, so most assignments may be redone to earn back half of the missing points. The only assignments ineligible for reworks are the final paper and presentation. We highly recommend that you don't let re-works slow you down. This class moves quickly. 

Finally, it is your responsibility to talk with your mentor to figure out what is expected of you. This course is designed to tailor to your skill level and should get progressively more difficult throughout the semester.
